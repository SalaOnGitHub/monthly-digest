Interest rates2026-10-28

The Fed holds rates as inflation stays elevated

The Federal Reserve kept its target interest-rate range at 3.75–4.00% on October 28. It described strong economic activity and resilient spending, while inflation remained elevated. The vote was 10–2; both dissenters preferred a quarter-point increase. The statement leaves future decisions dependent on additional information rather than promising a particular rate path.

WHY IT MATTERS

Rates influence financing costs, bond yields and the discount rates used to value stocks. A hold still needs to be read alongside the inflation outlook.

Global economy2026-10-13

The IMF sees uneven growth behind the global average

The IMF's October outlook projects global growth of 3.1% in 2026 and 3.4% in 2027, with inflation at 4.7% and 4.1% respectively. It highlights the divide between economies benefiting from technology investment and energy importers under pressure. Conflict, trade tensions, debt and a reassessment of AI expectations remain downside risks. These are forecasts, not realised outcomes.

WHY IT MATTERS

For stock and ETF investors, global averages can hide different country and sector exposures. The report provides scenarios for understanding risk rather than a market-return forecast.

Company results2026-10-28

Microsoft's earnings show why accounting definitions matter

For the quarter ended September 30, Microsoft reported revenue of $92.3 billion, up 19% year on year. Earnings per share rose 32% under GAAP, compared with 18% on its adjusted measure excluding OpenAI investment effects. Microsoft Cloud revenue increased 30%. The October 28 release illustrates how a headline growth figure can differ from the underlying comparison.

WHY IT MATTERS

Read the reconciliation as well as the headline. Company results can inform an assessment of business performance; they do not establish whether a stock is attractively priced.

Company results2026-10-15

TSMC reports strong chip demand and a higher sales outlook

TSMC reported third-quarter revenue of US$46.72 billion, up 41.2% from a year earlier, and a gross margin of 66.4%. Its October 15 results linked demand to leading-edge chip technologies. Management forecast fourth-quarter revenue of US$52.3–54.3 billion. The forecast describes the company's expectations and remains separate from the completed quarter's results.

WHY IT MATTERS

Chip demand is relevant to technology shares and ETFs holding semiconductor companies. Revenue growth, margins and the price already paid for expected growth need separate consideration.

ETF developments2026-10-14

New Xetra listings span low-cost indexes and active AI

Deutsche Börse's October 14 listing notice includes an Amundi Core MSCI World UCITS ETF share class with stated product costs of 0.06% a year. The same notice includes Pictet's actively managed AI Enhanced Emerging Equity UCITS ETF at 0.30%. Its AI tools support investment analysis; the fund remains an equity investment with market risk.

WHY IT MATTERS

An ETF is a wrapper, not one investment strategy. Compare the market covered, management approach and total costs before treating two ETFs as substitutes.

ETF developments2026-10-14

Thematic ETFs look to the infrastructure behind AI

In a notice published October 14, Amundi announced three thematic UCITS ETFs focused on space technologies, critical metals and rare earths, and electrification infrastructure. It scheduled their Xetra and Euronext Paris listings for October 20. The range targets physical enablers of technology, including grids, materials and space infrastructure, rather than only software companies.

A compelling theme can still mean concentrated exposure. The index rules, holdings, valuation and overlap with existing funds matter more than the theme's name alone.

DiversificationBackgroundInvestor lens

Investor lens: what does a global index actually cover?

MSCI World covers large and mid-sized companies in developed markets. Its name does not mean every country or company is included: emerging markets and small companies require a wider or different index universe. A fund can hold many stocks while retaining substantial exposure to particular countries, sectors or its largest holdings.

WHY IT MATTERS

Check the index definition and holdings before counting funds as diversification. Owning two similar ETFs may repeat much of the same exposure.

Costs & tradingBackgroundInvestor lens

Investor lens: ETF costs extend beyond the annual fee

The annual fund fee is only one component of investing costs. Brokerage charges and the bid–ask spread also affect a purchase or sale. An ETF's exchange price can differ from its net asset value. A limit order controls the worst acceptable execution price, but it may remain unfilled if the market never reaches that limit.

WHY IT MATTERS

Small recurring purchases can be sensitive to fixed trading fees. Comparing ongoing charges with trading costs gives a more complete picture than comparing one fee number.

Currency exposureBackgroundInvestor lens

Investor lens: a euro listing does not remove currency risk

An ETF's trading currency tells you how a particular exchange quotation is priced. It does not change the underlying investments. A euro-traded fund holding overseas shares can therefore still be affected by exchange-rate movements. Currency-hedged share classes use a different mechanism and need to be identified explicitly; a EUR ticker alone does not establish a hedge.

WHY IT MATTERS

Separate the currency used to place the order from the currencies behind the portfolio. This helps explain why an index's return and your euro return may differ.

EU fund documentsBackgroundInvestor lens

Investor lens: use the KID to compare funds

The EU's PRIIPs key information document, or KID, summarises a packaged investment product in up to three pages. It covers its intended investors, risk and reward profile, potential loss, performance scenarios and costs. Read it alongside the fund factsheet and prospectus to understand what the product is designed to do.

WHY IT MATTERS

A consistent document makes it easier to compare products beyond advertising and past returns. Scenarios describe possible outcomes; they are not a promise of future performance.

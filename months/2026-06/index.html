OpenAI Unveils Its First Custom Chip, Jalapeño

On June 24, OpenAI revealed Jalapeño, an ASIC built with Broadcom and designed purely for AI inference — the work of running ChatGPT responses and Codex agents rather than training. Broadcom CEO Hock Tan says it matches Nvidia's Blackwell GPUs and Google's TPUs on performance, helping OpenAI cut its dependence on scarce Nvidia hardware. The company calls it the "first step in a multi-generation compute platform," expects to deploy it by the end of 2026, and says early testing shows substantially better performance per watt.

Anthropic's Claude Tag Brings an AI Agent Into Slack

On June 23, Anthropic introduced Claude Tag, an integration that lets Claude join a company's Slack workspace as a member you summon by typing @Claude. Once tagged, it can write and merge pull requests, locate sales numbers, analyze data, and take on delegated tasks directly inside the conversations where work already happens. The agent learns the organization over time, pushing coding assistants out of the IDE and into everyday team chat.

Sakana's Fugu Is a Model That Routes to Other Models

Japanese AI lab Sakana released Fugu, a system that posts benchmark results rivaling or beating Fable 5, Gemini 3.1 Pro, and GPT-5.5 — not by being one big model, but by routing each task to the best frontier model available, including Claude and Gemini. The catch: Fugu doesn't tell users which model handled their request, and Sakana says it will "naturally grow" by folding in newer, more efficient models, including its own. It is a concrete bet that orchestration, not a single set of weights, is the next competitive layer.

4 of 10

Google DeepMind Publishes an AI Control Roadmap

On June 19, Google DeepMind released an AI Control Roadmap for keeping increasingly autonomous agents in check — described as a "driving instructor with dual controls" who trusts the student but stays ready to take the wheel or hit the brakes. The plan lays out internal guardrails to catch adversarial behavior even as agents become harder to oversee, naming methods like chain-of-thought monitoring, asynchronous alerts, real-time access control, and shutdown infrastructure. It is one of the first public engineering blueprints for containing agents that act on their own.

Five Eyes Warns Organizations to Act Now on AI Cyber Threats

On June 22, the Five Eyes intelligence alliance, via the UK's National Cyber Security Centre, warned that AI models are set to fundamentally transform offensive and defensive cyber capabilities within months. The guidance is blunt: "breaches will occur" as previously unknown vulnerabilities emerge, and "adversaries are already using AI to move faster and more effectively." Its message to security leaders is to act now — defenders must adopt AI tooling at the same pace attackers already have.

OpenAI Launches a Drive to Find and Patch Open-Source Bugs

On June 22, OpenAI announced a new initiative that points its AI agents at widely used open-source projects to uncover security flaws and propose fixes. The effort mirrors a growing belief that the same models capable of writing exploits can also harden the code the internet runs on, scanning dependencies most maintainers lack the resources to audit. It lands the same week the Five Eyes alliance urged defenders to embrace AI, framing automated bug-hunting as a frontline defense for the software supply chain.

The Hidden Security Cost of Vibe-Coded Apps

A June 22 investigation detailed how the boom in vibe coding is shipping a wave of insecure software: researchers found roughly 5,000 publicly accessible AI-built apps with no authentication, nearly 2,000 of them leaking medical, financial, or strategy data. One viral app exposed its entire production database within days of launch. Experts urge builders to prompt for security up front, review every patch the AI writes, and treat the move from local machine to the cloud as the moment the stakes change.

CrankGPT Runs a Local LLM on Hand-Cranked Power

A two-person team at Squeez Labs unveiled CrankGPT, an LLM-in-a-box that runs private, local AI models with no power-hungry data center behind it. The twist: you physically crank a generator to produce the electricity for every single answer, turning each prompt into a small workout. Spotted by The Register, the concept project is a pointed jab at AI's ballooning energy footprint — and proof that inference doesn't have to live in the cloud.

9 of 10

The Atlantic Makes AI's Music Training Data Searchable

Atlantic reporter Alex Reisner uncovered four datasets used to train music-generating AI and made them fully searchable to the public — two of them holding a staggering 12 million and 9 million tracks. Downloaded thousands of times and used by Google and Stability per their own papers, the sets sweep up artists from Radiohead to Wu-Tang Clan to Bruce Springsteen, often via tools that strip YouTube and Spotify audio in violation of their terms. The exposé fueled a backlash, with SZA blasting AI-supporting musicians as "disgusting" after finding 238 of her songs inside.

Apple Details Practical AI Features Coming in iOS 27

Ahead of its fall release, Apple laid out the practical AI features arriving in iOS 27, moving past a long-delayed Siri overhaul toward smaller tools people will actually use day to day. The update threads Apple Intelligence deeper into core apps — across messages, photos, and on-device assistance — while leaning on its privacy-first, on-device approach. After a year of stumbles, it is a more grounded pitch focused on useful, shippable features over flashy demos.

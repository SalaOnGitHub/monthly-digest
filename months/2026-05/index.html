Google Reinvents Search at I/O 2026

At Google I/O on May 19, Google declared the era of the ten blue links officially over, unveiling an AI-powered overhaul built on Gemini Flash 3.5. The new Search drops users into interactive experiences, dispatches background information agents to monitor the web 24/7, and lets users build personal mini apps with natural-language commands. AI Overviews already reach 2.5 billion monthly users, and the generative UI overhaul begins rolling out free to all this summer.

2 of 10

Gemini Omni: Input Anything, Output Anything

Also at I/O, Google introduced Gemini Omni, a new family of models designed to convert any input — photo, video, or text — into any other format. Omni Flash, the first release, is now available inside Google's AI video platform Flow and improves on the previous Veo model with better character consistency and deeper real-world knowledge during generation. The model can accept an existing video as a starting point alongside a text prompt, moving video creation from novelty to routine workflow.

SpaceX Files for the Largest IPO in History

SpaceX made its S-1 public on May 20, targeting a $1.75 trillion valuation and roughly $75 billion raised under the Nasdaq ticker SPCX. Elon Musk will serve as CEO, CTO, and board chairman after the listing, holding 85.1% of voting power. xAI — which includes Grok — was already merged into SpaceX, and the filing reveals SpaceX directed 60% of its 2025 capital spending, around $20 billion, to its AI division, which still lost billions while growing revenue by only 22%.

Anthropic Is Paying SpaceX $15 Billion a Year for Compute

SpaceX's IPO filing revealed that Anthropic agreed to pay $1.25 billion per month through May 2029 for access to the Colossus I and Colossus II data centers in Memphis — $15 billion annually, nearly matching SpaceX's entire 2025 revenue. Either party can exit with 90 days' notice, a clause that reflects both the speed of AI development and the political oddity of Claude's maker funding Grok's parent company. Anthropic is also separately in talks to rent Microsoft Azure servers as its hunger for compute outpaces any single provider.

5 of 10

Anthropic Nears Its First Profitable Quarter

Anthropic told investors it expects to more than double revenue to $10.9 billion in Q2 2026 and deliver an operating profit for the first time, according to the Wall Street Journal. Profitability may not hold throughout the year given the massive compute costs ahead — including the Colossus deal — but the milestone puts Anthropic in a stronger competitive position against OpenAI, which the same week was reported to be preparing its own IPO filing for as early as September. Anthropic declined to comment.

Andrej Karpathy Joins Anthropic to Lead Pre-Training Research

Andrej Karpathy — OpenAI co-founder, former head of Tesla's Autopilot program, and one of the world's most recognized AI researchers — joined Anthropic on May 19 to work on pre-training under team lead Nick Joseph. He will build a team focused on using Claude to accelerate pre-training research itself, a sign that Anthropic believes AI-assisted science, not just raw compute, is how it stays competitive at the frontier. The hire came the same day Anthropic disclosed it would hit its first profitable quarter.

7 of 10

Sam Altman Offers Every YC Startup $2M in OpenAI Tokens

At a Y Combinator event on May 20, Sam Altman made what YC partner Tyler Bosmeny called a "mic drop moment": $2 million in OpenAI tokens as uncapped SAFEs to every startup in the Spring 2026 cohort of roughly 169 companies. The deal locks startups into OpenAI's platform at a critical early stage, blunting adoption of Anthropic's Claude Code and other competitors. Critics including seed investor Jason Calacanis warned it is the "classic platform playbook" — study the startups, copy the ideas, then roll them into a free offering.

8 of 10

Nvidia Posts $81.6 Billion Quarter and Eyes a New $200B Market

Nvidia reported record Q1 FY2027 results on May 20: overall revenue of $81.6 billion and data center revenue of $75.2 billion, up 92% from the same period last year. CEO Jensen Huang told investors Nvidia has identified a "brand new" $200 billion market it plans to enter, and confirmed its next-generation Vera Rubin AI chip is on track for Q3 2026. PC sales are softer due to a RAM shortage, but the data center juggernaut showed no sign of slowing as AI infrastructure spending intensifies.

9 of 10

Meta and Intuit Lead a New Wave of AI-Justified Layoffs

Meta laid off thousands of employees in May, with an internal memo explicitly framing the cuts as necessary to offset the company's rising AI investment costs. Days earlier, Intuit cut roughly 3,000 employees — 17% of its global workforce — to refocus capital on AI-powered products, per a CEO memo seen by Reuters. The pattern is accelerating: after last year's waves tied to efficiency, companies are now explicitly invoking AI spending as the reason for headcount reductions, normalizing a trade-off between human jobs and model training budgets.

Pope Leo's Encyclical: Stay Profoundly Human in the Age of AI

Pope Leo XIV published his first major manifesto on May 25, calling for a new legal and ethical framework to govern AI and urging humanity to remain "profoundly human" as the technology reshapes labor, warfare, and daily life. The document, an encyclical, references Anthropic by name and addresses autonomous weapons, job displacement, and the dignity of workers. It is the first time a sitting pope has issued a formal doctrinal statement dedicated to AI, putting the moral weight of the Catholic Church's 1.4 billion followers behind the call for stronger international oversight.

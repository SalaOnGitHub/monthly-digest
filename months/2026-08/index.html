Astra Settles Ten Math Problems That Stood for Decades

On August 2, OpenAI revealed that an internal version of Astra had produced new results for ten problems in mathematics and theoretical computer science that had been open for at least a decade — among them an explicit non-sofic group, settling a question left hanging since Mikhail Gromov introduced soficity in 1999. The lab published a 249-page manuscript alongside Lean 4 proof certificates on GitHub, where the count of unproven steps stands at zero, for roughly $2,000 in compute. None of the ten has been through peer review, and the model itself remains unreleased.

Demis Hassabis Steps Aside at Google DeepMind

On August 5, Demis Hassabis handed over day-to-day control of Google DeepMind, becoming the unit's chairman and chief scientist of Alphabet so he can concentrate on AGI strategy and the drug discovery spinoff Isomorphic Labs. CTO Koray Kavukcuoglu takes over operations as senior vice president, reporting to Sundar Pichai rather than holding the CEO title. The change caps a summer of departures: Jeff Dean left after 27 years to co-found Discovery Loop with Sanjay Ghemawat, Oriol Vinyals and Quoc Le, while Noam Shazeer went to OpenAI and Nobel laureate John Jumper to Anthropic.

Meta Open-Weights a Model That Runs on Your Laptop

On August 10, Meta released Muse Glimmer, a 30-billion-parameter model under an Apache 2.0 license that runs locally on a Mac or PC with a single consumer GPU, with or without an internet connection. Distilled from the closed Muse Spark, it is built for multi-step agent work — tool use, coding, file handling and reading screenshots. Zuckerberg framed it as "personal superintelligence" that individuals own rather than rent, a pointed reversal after Meta's drift toward closed models earlier in the year.

Claude Starts Watermarking Everything It Writes

Anthropic explained on August 14 how Claude now marks its own output, a direct answer to the EU AI Act's Article 50, which became enforceable on August 2 and requires generative systems to label what they produce in machine-readable form. The watermark changes the source of randomness Claude uses when choosing between equally good words, leaving a pattern invisible to readers but detectable to anyone holding the key; generated files also carry C2PA content credentials. Anthropic is candid about the limits — it cannot separate authorship from heavy editing, and it weakens on short or factual passages where wording is constrained.

A Backflipping Robot Maker Becomes a Blue Chip

On August 19, Unitree became the first humanoid robot maker listed in mainland China, pricing at 150.8 yuan and opening at 1,100 — a 629% pop that briefly valued it at US$66 billion. It handed much of that back the same day, closing at 845 yuan, still up 460% on its offer price. Founded in Hangzhou in 2016, Unitree is best known for machines that perform backflips and martial arts on China's Spring Festival gala.

6 of 10

Beijing, Not Washington, Now Caps Nvidia's China Sales

Nvidia's second-quarter filing showed its China problem had changed shape. It took a $400 million charge on excess H200 inventory as demand fell away, and sales of the chip to Chinese buyers came to less than 1% of the $89 billion it booked in data center revenue. The company's own wording was blunt — such sales "were restricted by the PRC government, and we have been unable to sell all the products for which we have licenses" — leaving Beijing, not Washington, as the binding constraint.

Anthropic Passes OpenAI on Revenue and Turns a Profit

Internal documents reported on August 15 showed Anthropic booked $11.5 billion in second-quarter revenue, up roughly 14x year over year and 2.4x from the previous quarter, along with its first positive adjusted operating income at about $559 million. For the first time the company outearned OpenAI, which posted $6.7 billion over the same period. The word carrying the weight is "adjusted" — and Anthropic expects to drop back into losses as it keeps buying compute.

8 of 10

Sony and Warner Chappell Sue Anthropic, Naming Its Founders

Late on August 29, Sony Music Publishing and Warner Chappell filed suit in the Northern District of California, accusing Anthropic of "a brazen campaign of illegally torrenting, scraping, and downloading copyrighted works" to train Claude. Unusually, the complaint names Dario Amodei and Benjamin Mann personally alongside the company, reaching past the corporate shield to its founders. Anthropic's reply was short: "We disagree with the publishers' claims and we intend to defend ourselves robustly in court."

9 of 10

The Pentagon Puts ChatGPT and Grok on Its Own AI Portal

On August 31, the Defense Department added ChatGPT Mil and Grok for Government to GenAI.mil, the internal platform that launched with Google Gemini and has now onboarded more than 1.7 million of the department's 3 million personnel. One name is conspicuously missing: Anthropic's Claude. The Trump administration labeled the company a supply-chain risk after it declined to give the Pentagon unrestricted access and insisted on keeping its safety guardrails in place.

Europe Starts Regulating ChatGPT as a Search Engine

Also on August 31, the European Commission designated ChatGPT a Very Large Online Search Engine under the Digital Services Act — the first AI chatbot ever given the label. Scale is the trigger: the threshold is 45 million monthly users in the EU, and OpenAI reported roughly 159.1 million for ChatGPT search. Within four months it must run systemic risk assessments, submit to annual independent audits, open data to vetted researchers and offer a recommender option that is not based on profiling.
